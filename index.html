Pangenotypic DAA SVR12 >95% HBV / HIV co-infection

Diagnostic algorithm

Anti-HCV antibody is the screening test. HCV RNA confirms active infection. A negative antibody does not exclude acute infection in the first weeks after exposure.

Who to screen

Universal one-time screening for all adults ≥18 years; every pregnant woman each pregnancy.

Risk-based indications

  • Persons who inject drugs (current or ever), or intranasal drug use
  • Blood products / organ transplant before 1992; clotting factor before 1987
  • Haemodialysis, CKD stage 5
  • Needlestick / sharps injury, mucosal exposure to HCV-positive blood
  • Children born to HCV-positive mothers (RNA at 2–6 months or antibody ≥18 months)
  • HIV, HBV, unexplained chronic ALT elevation
  • Incarceration, MSM, transgender persons

Non-invasive fibrosis staging

Biopsy not required. Use FIB-4 first; confirm indeterminate results with transient elastography or a serum panel.

Regimen selection

Genotype is not required for pangenotypic regimens. Co-infection does not change the core HCV regimen — it changes monitoring and DDI review (see next tab).

HBV & HIV co-infection management

Core principle: treat HCV with the same pangenotypic DAA regimen as mono-infection. Co-infection changes monitoring and drug-interaction review — not the HCV regimen itself.

HBV serostatus

HBV DNA >2000 IU/mL + ALT >ULN, or significant fibrosis, or cirrhosis

DAA adverse-effect profile

Modern DAAs are well tolerated. Most AEs are mild and non-specific. Class-specific effects and ribavirin drive most clinically significant toxicity.

Class-specific effects

NS3/4A protease inhibitors — the "-previrs"

Glecaprevir · Grazoprevir · Voxilaprevir · (older: simeprevir, paritaprevir/ritonavir)
  • Hyperbilirubinaemia and ALT elevations — usually mild and reversible; monitor LFTs if cirrhosis
  • Contraindicated in decompensated (Child-Pugh B/C) cirrhosis — risk of severe hepatotoxicity
  • Simeprevir (withdrawn): photosensitivity / rash
  • Paritaprevir/ritonavir (withdrawn): ritonavir-mediated CYP3A interactions with many drugs
  • DDI caution: strong P-gp/BCRP inhibitors (e.g. cyclosporine >100 mg/day), rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John's wort

NS5A inhibitors — the "-asvirs"

Pibrentasvir · Velpatasvir · Ledipasvir · Elbasvir · (older: daclatasvir, ombitasvir)
  • Generally the best-tolerated class — headache, fatigue, nausea only
  • Absorption reduced by acid-suppressing agents — ledipasvir and velpatasvir are the most pH-sensitive. Separate antacids by 4 h; PPIs should be taken with food at the same time as the DAA, or avoided
  • No dose adjustment in renal impairment
  • Grazoprevir/elbasvir (withdrawn) required baseline NS5A RAS testing for genotype 1a

NS5B inhibitor — sofosbuvir

Sofosbuvir (Sovaldi) · also in SOF/VEL, SOF/LDV, SOF/DCV, SOF/VEL/VOX
  • Well tolerated as monotherapy backbone
  • Older labelling: not recommended with eGFR <30 mL/min. Current Sovaldi labelling now supports use across genotypes and CKD stages as part of combination therapy — no dose adjustment needed
  • Real-world cohorts show small declines in eGFR and haemoglobin, mild rises in uric acid and LDL
  • Amiodarone + sofosbuvir → serious symptomatic bradycardia — avoid, or monitor inpatient for 48 h if unavoidable

Ribavirin — the main driver of toxicity

Added to SOF/VEL in decompensated cirrhosis, genotype 3 salvage, and some retreatment regimens
  • Haemolytic anaemia — dose-dependent; check Hb at baseline and week 4, then as clinically indicated. Reduce by 200 mg steps if Hb <10 g/dL; stop if <8.5 g/dL
  • Cough, rash, insomnia, irritability
  • Teratogenic — absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy. Effective contraception required during therapy and for 6 months after in both partners
  • Ribavirin-containing regimens (especially SOF/RBV) show markedly higher anaemia and hyperbilirubinaemia than ribavirin-free combinations
  • In Child-Pugh C: start at 600 mg/day and titrate up as tolerated
🚩
Stop treatment and investigate if: ALT rises >10× ULN, or ALT rises with jaundice, dark urine, nausea, fatigue or eosinophilia. These may indicate DAA-induced hepatotoxicity, HBV reactivation, or another intercurrent liver injury.
